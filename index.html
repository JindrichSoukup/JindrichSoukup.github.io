Nálezník (Findings Herbarium)

App interface is in Czech.

A field-planning tool for iNaturalist's Česká flóra project: tell it where and when you'll be, and it shows which of your still-missing vascular plant species you could realistically find nearby, with real GPS localities, flowering windows, and warnings for species that are usually garden plants rather than wild ones. Built from iNaturalist, Pladias, and GBIF data, packaged as a single offline-capable HTML file — no install, no backend.

Magazine RAG (Živa archive pipeline)

Seventy-five years of a natural-history magazine exist as one PDF per issue: readable, but not searchable. Ask "what has Živa published about mycorrhiza" and the only honest answer is "open forty files and hope." This turns that archive into a corpus you can query — article boundaries recovered from the printed contents page, printed page numbers mapped back to PDF pages, hyphenated line breaks stitched together, figure captions kept out of the running text — then embedded, indexed, and answered from with citations down to the issue and page. The interesting part is not the vector search; it is everything before it.

I built it in two passes, and the second one is the point. The first pass was by hand, one layer at a time, to find out what a retrieval system actually decides — and what it decides silently for you when you aren't looking. That produced two notes worth more than the code: a layer-by-layer map of every decision a RAG system makes, and what hands-on building says about build-vs-buy when someone else has to run it. The second pass generalised the pipeline into swappable source profiles and moved it onto an unrelated magazine (The MagPi, CC BY-NC-SA) — 85 articles from three issues with no hand-tuned typography. Porting it surfaced five bugs the original magazine never could, and not one of them crashed anything: the pipeline finished every time and printed a contented summary. Every article it produces carries explicit flags where it had to guess.
